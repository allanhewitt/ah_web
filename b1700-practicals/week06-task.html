Describing our Data

Pair Task Sheet · Core → Audit → Workflow → Transfer → Extension

WHY THIS MATTERS

Central question: What does the evidence show, and how should we represent it?

A summary or a plot is a choice. It can be correct and still hide a group, exaggerate a gap or suggest a cause, so every summary needs a question before it and a careful reading after it: QUESTION → VARIABLES → SUMMARY OR PLOT → DESCRIBE → CHECK WHAT IT HIDES → SAY.

How to work this week

In the demonstration you summarised minutes_played, drew a histogram, a boxplot and a scatterplot, saw how a bin width or a truncated axis can mislead, and followed a rowing coach’s three files from the first look to a careful sentence. Here you apply the same moves to distance_km and sprints, judge a colleague’s summary, work through a cycling coach’s three files yourselves, and then try a swimming file.

Phase 1 revisits Weeks 1 to 5 and rebuilds the table you will describe, because a description is only as good as the table under it.

Work through this page in order as a pair, in your own script (week06_session.R has a heading for each task). Keep brief comments as you go, so that your script records both what you ran and what you concluded. Most tasks start from a block in week06_demo.R.

Predict before you run. Write down what you expect to see before every summary and every plot, and compare it with the result. Describe a pattern before you explain it.

If your pair gets stuck, open the Week 6 Hint Sheet and use the hint for the relevant task. Use as little support as you need, then return to the task. Phases 1 to 5 are the main work of the session. Phase 6 asks you to transfer the process to different data, and Phase 7 is for pairs who want to go further.

PHASE 1: CORE: REVISIT WEEKS 1 TO 5

Rebuild and check the table before you describe it.

Task 1.1: Restart and import

Use Session > Restart R so that nothing from the demonstration is left in memory. Check that week_six.Rproj is the open project. Start a new script, save it, and load the tidyverse. Keep week06_demo.R open in another tab: most tasks start from a block of it.

As a pair: Import three files from the data folder: player_match_tracking.csv into tracking, match_results.csv into matches and players.csv into players. (The swimming file is imported later.) Say what one row represents in each table.

Task 1.2: Check tracking from memory

As a pair: Without looking at an earlier script, write the code that gives you: a first look at tracking; its number of rows and columns; the number of missing values in match_rating; the smallest and largest value of minutes_played and distance_km; the number of exact duplicate rows; and a count of the rows for each position.

Record: What needs attention before you describe anything in this table?

Task 1.3: Rebuild and verify the joined table

As a pair: Build tracking_joined, which adds only player_name from players and only result from matches to tracking, keeping every appearance. Predict the numbers of rows and columns first. Then verify the result: the numbers of rows and columns, the missing player_name and result values, whether any player appears twice in the same match, and an anti_join() from tracking to each lookup table.

Record: Expected and observed counts, and a final statement of what one row represents. A joined table is not ready to describe until it has been checked.

PHASE 2: CORE: DESCRIBE WITH NUMBERS

Predict, calculate, then say what each number leaves out.

Task 2.1: Centre and spread of distance_km

As a pair: Predict whether the mean of distance_km will be above, below or about equal to the median. Then calculate the mean, median, standard deviation, IQR and quantiles for all 476 appearances.

Record: Your prediction, the five numbers, and one sentence on what the mean and median together suggest.

Task 2.2: A summary for each position

As a pair: Build a table with one row for each position, showing the number of appearances and the mean, median, standard deviation and IQR of distance_km.

Record: The table, and a sentence naming the positions that are most alike and the position that stands apart.

Task 2.3: Does one number describe the squad?

As a pair: Calculate the mean distance_km for outfield players only, and the largest distance_km recorded by a goalkeeper. Then count how many of the 476 appearances lie within 1 km of the overall mean (the overall mean plus or minus 1 km), and how many of those are goalkeeper appearances.

Record: The numbers, and a sentence on how well the overall mean describes a goalkeeper appearance and an outfield appearance.

Task 2.4: Ratings and the values behind them

As a pair: First calculate the mean of match_rating for the whole table without any option and note what you get. Then, for each position, calculate the number of appearances, the number of appearances that have a rating, and the mean rating.

Record: What the first calculation returned and why, the table, and one sentence on why the number of ratings belongs next to each mean.

PHASE 3: CORE: DRAW IT

Choose the plot from the question and the variables, and describe what you see before you explain it.

Task 3.1: One numeric variable: histogram

As a pair: Draw a histogram of distance_km with binwidth = 1. Then draw it again with binwidth = 0.5. Before you explain anything, describe the shape: where the centre is, how wide the spread is and whether there is one group of values or more.

Record: Your description of the shape in one or two sentences, and whether the second bin width changed your description.

Task 3.2: Numeric and category: boxplot

As a pair: Draw a boxplot of distance_km for each position. Compare the medians, the heights of the boxes and any separate points.

Record: A sentence on how the positions differ in centre and in spread, and a sentence on what the separate points are. Link the plot to the histogram you drew in Task 3.1.

Task 3.3: Two numeric variables: scatterplot

As a pair: Draw a scatterplot with distance_km on the horizontal axis and sprints on the vertical axis. Add a title, a subtitle that says what one point is, axis labels with units, and theme_minimal(). Describe the direction, strength and scatter of the pattern, and anything about the points that is not a single cloud.

Record: Your description in terms of direction, strength and scatter, and one thing you notice that you cannot yet explain.

Task 3.4: Say it carefully

As a pair: Write two sentences for the coaching staff: one on how distance covered is distributed and how positions compare, and one on the relationship between distance and sprints. Each sentence must include at least one number, say how many appearances it rests on, and avoid words that claim a cause.

Record: Your two sentences.

PHASE 4: AUDIT: JUDGE A COLLEAGUE’S SUMMARY

Check what the code shows, not what the note says it shows.

Task 4.1: What does each step actually show?

As a pair: Open colleague_summary.R (read it, but do not edit it). Run it one step at a time. For each of the three claims in the handover note, write down what the code produces: the table, the correlation and the number of bars.

Record: A table with three rows and the headings Claim, What the code shows, and Supported?.

Task 4.2: Verdict

As a pair: For each claim, decide whether the evidence supports it, partly supports it or does not support it, and give the reason. Then say what you would change so that the summary is defensible.

Record: Three verdicts with reasons, and the changes you would make.

PHASE 5: WORKFLOW: ONE CASE, START TO FINISH

Three new files and one question. Understand, trust, clean, connect, transform, describe.

Task 5.1: Understand and trust the files

As a pair: A cycling coach has sent three files and one question: which kind of ride covers the ground fastest, and does the discipline make a difference? Import week06_task_riders.csv into riders, week06_task_rides.csv into rides and week06_task_tests.csv into tests. Say what one row represents in each file. Then check rides: its numbers of rows and columns, the missing values in each column, and the range of minutes and distance_km. Work out the speed of the ride with the largest distance.

Record: The grain of each file, the counts, and a list of what needs a decision before you describe anything.

Task 5.2: Clean the labels and the repeats

As a pair: Count the labels in discipline and bring them down to the two that exist. Check for exact duplicate rows, and for the same rider on the same date more than once, and decide what to do about each. Decide what to do about the ride that cannot be right. Store the cleaned table as rides_clean and report the number of rows after each step. Then reshape tests so that there is one row per rider per test, and state what one row is now.

Record: The label counts before and after, the row count after each step, your decision about the impossible ride and the reason, and the new grain of tests.

Task 5.3: Connect the tables, then verify

As a pair: Before you join, find the rides whose rider is not in riders and the riders who have no rides. Then build rides_joined, which adds rider_name to every ride in rides_clean without losing any. Predict the number of rows first. Check the number of rows, and the number of rides left without a name. Finally, find out how many rows inner_join() would have returned instead.

Record: Expected and observed counts, the unmatched records in both directions, and one sentence on what the other join would have done to the data.

Task 5.4: Transform: a total or a rate?

As a pair: Add a column speed_kmh to rides_joined equal to distance_km / minutes * 60. For each ride_type, calculate the number of rides, the mean distance_km and the mean speed_kmh. Which type covers more distance in a ride, and which covers it faster?

Record: The table, and a sentence on why the two questions have different answers.

Task 5.5: Describe and draw

As a pair: Using rides_joined: (a) predict whether the mean of minutes is above or below its median, then calculate the number of rides, mean, median, standard deviation and IQR. (b) Draw a histogram of minutes and name its shape. (c) Draw a boxplot of speed_kmh for each discipline and compare the centre, the spread and the overlap. (d) Draw a scatterplot of avg_power_w against speed_kmh with a title, a subtitle, axis labels with units and theme_minimal(), and describe direction, strength and scatter. Note any warning R gives you.

Record: Your prediction and the numbers, the shape you named, one sentence for each of the boxplot and the scatterplot, and the number of rides each rests on.

Task 5.6: Say it carefully

As a pair: Write two sentences for the coach. The first compares Road and Track riding on speed_kmh. The second reports the relationship between power and speed. Each must include a number, say how many rides it rests on, and avoid words that claim a cause.

Record: Your two sentences.

PHASE 6: TRANSFER

Different data, very different scales and the same judgement process.

Task 6.1: Check before you describe

As a pair: Import week06_transfer_swimming.csv into swimming. State what one row represents. Check the numbers of rows and columns, the number of swimmers in each event_group, any missing values, and the range of recent_training_load and race_time_seconds.

Record: The grain, the counts and what you notice about the range of race times.

Task 6.2: The pooled picture

As a pair: Predict whether more recent training load will go with faster or slower race times. Then draw a scatterplot of recent_training_load (horizontal) against race_time_seconds (vertical) for all 18 swimmers, with a title, axis labels and theme_minimal().

Record: Your prediction, what the plot shows, and whether it matched.

Task 6.3: Compare like with like

As a pair: Draw the same scatterplot with a separate panel for each event_group, and give each panel its own vertical scale. Calculate the correlation between recent_training_load and race_time_seconds within each event group. Compare what you see with Task 6.2.

Record: What each panel shows, the three correlations, and why the pooled picture and the panels disagree.

Task 6.4: Report it

As a pair: Write one or two sentences for a coach saying what the 18 swimmers show about training load and race time, and what they cannot show.

Record: Your sentences.

PHASE 7: EXTENSION

Look further at structure you have already noticed, and at what else R can draw.

Task 7.1: Sprints by venue

As a pair: In Task 3.3 the points did not form a single cloud. Draw a histogram of sprints (binwidth = 2) with a separate panel for each venue. Then calculate, for each venue, the number of appearances, the median and IQR of sprints, and the number of appearances with 10 or fewer sprints and with 24 or more.

Record: What you see in each venue, and what you would ask the data owner before using a mean number of sprints.

Task 7.2: A reusable summary

As a pair: Write a function describe_num(data, column) that returns a one-row table with the number of rows, the number of missing values, and the mean, median, standard deviation and IQR of column, ignoring missing values in the calculations. Test it on distance_km and on match_rating.

Record: The function and its two results, and one sentence on why it reports the missing count.

Task 7.3: See what else R can draw

As a pair: Open week06_demo.R and go to the section headed Where this goes near the bottom, which starts at library(ggridges). If R asks, install the two extra packages once by typing install.packages(c("ggridges", "ggrepel")) in the Console. Run it one plot at a time. Choose the plot you find most striking, find the lines that make it, and change one thing: a colour, a label or a number.

Record: The plot you chose, what you changed and what happened, and one question about sport that this kind of plot could help you answer.

PHASE 8: DEBRIEF

Connect describing and communicating with professional judgement.

Task 8.1: What does your plot hide?

As a pair: Choose one plot you drew today. What does it make easy to see, and what does it hide? Name one further summary, plot or table that you would add to show what it hides.

Task 8.2: Which stage mattered most?

As a pair: Look back at the cycling case in Phase 5. For each of the six stages (understand, trust, clean, connect, transform, describe), say what you checked and whether it changed what you could honestly say.

Record: Your six short answers, and the stage you would least want to skip.
